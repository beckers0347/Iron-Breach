## 0. What changed from the original M2 draft (read this first)

The original `M2_THE_FENCE_Mission_Design.md` was a coastal exclusion-zone patrol: walk the fence, attend a Pilgrim vigil, repel a probing Class D stray at a deterrent pylon, escort a Meridian salvage survey to PALAWAN's corpse, and end on a distant SIRENA sighting. That mission is **retired outright**, per your direction — M2 is now the search for the garrison's lost mech.

**The new mission, one sentence:** nine days (or however long feels right — see §1) after Landfall, the squad hikes into the mountains the helicopter glimpsed on the way out, finds Vance's body at the wreck, talks a cornered and grieving Achterberg down from a fight, and gets the crippled mech walking again well enough to bring it — and her — home. That walk out is the player's first hands-on time with mech controls.

**What happens to the retired content:** the fence/vigil/Kettler/Aveiro/SIRENA/pylon material isn't deleted from the franchise, just displaced — it was carrying real weight (Q4/Q5/Q10 seeds, the Field Recovery Tag system's introduction, the Meridian-foreknowledge plant, the corpse-as-geology economy). §8 below lists exactly what got displaced and flags it as an open question for where it lands (a later mission, or folded into M3). **Not decided in this doc — needs your call.**

## 2. What M2 must accomplish

**Narrative:**
1. Close the loop M1 opened: confirm Vance's death, recover his body with the same dignity the game gave Ms. Idris, and give Achterberg a proper introduction as a person before she becomes a fixture of the player's cockpit.
2. Establish the copilot relationship's actual texture: Achterberg is not relieved to be found. She is armed, has been alone with what happened for over a week, and the squad has to earn her trust in real time, not have it handed to them because they're the good guys.
3. **[v2]** Literalize the "empty seat" as narrative and mechanical: the player takes the gunnery/systems position Vance died in. Nobody needs to say this out loud — the game says it by putting the player's hands where his were.

**Mechanical:**
1. Teach basic mech movement and systems (walk, turn, camera/look, a diagnostics/status readout) — diegetically, as "getting a crippled mech to walk itself out," not as a menu tutorial. Combat systems (weapons, targeting, the sync/clasp UI layer from later mech content) are explicitly **not** taught here — see §5.
2. Teach de-escalation as a real player-facing mechanic, not just a cutscene: talking Achterberg down should ask something of the player (holding fire, lowering a weapon, a dialogue/approach choice), not just play a scripted line and move on.
3. Reinforce the carry-adjacent design law from M1: no health bar, no score, no fail state on anything emotionally load-bearing (finding Vance's body, the Achterberg standoff).

**Franchise:**
1. First appearance of a player-operable mech, eleven missions ahead of the original plan (M6's "Two Hearts" was slated as the frame tutorial) — this needs reconciling; flagged in §13.
2. Establishes that the mech program has a human cost from the very first one the player touches — the player's first mech isn't a shiny unlock, it's a grave marker they're asked to get walking again.

## 4. Structure — four beats

### 4.1 THE SEARCH (~10 min)

**Open:** carried directly from M1's closing beat — the helicopter's brief glimpse of the crash site is the mission's premise; no new establishing scene needed, just a jump to "nine days later" and the squad forming up for the hike in. Rhodes briefs at the tree line: find the mech, recover anyone aboard, assess if it's salvageable.

**Playable content:** trail movement through mountain forest — a tonal reset after M1's urban collapse, and after the retired draft's fence-line routine. This is the campaign's first quiet, green, unpopulated space. Sprint/mantle over deadfall, basic tracking (broken branches, a scorched line through the canopy visible from below) as an interaction/observation loop rather than a marked waypoint — echoes Idris's "no marker" carry design philosophy in a lower-stakes way.

**Exit condition:** the trail's damage gets worse — bigger breaks, scorched earth instead of scattered branches — and the squad knows they're close.

### 4.2 DEBRIS FIELD (~8 min)

**Playable content:** the ballistic trail itself — torn canopy overhead, a long scorched furrow where the mech first hit and skidded, scattered armor plating and equipment. **Deliberate mirror of M1's "the birds are gone" motif**, recontextualized: the forest here isn't quiet because something kaiju-adjacent is wrong, it's quiet because a several-hundred-ton machine came down through it nine days ago and everything that could leave, did. No dialogue needed to sell this — environment only, same design law as M1's DREAD beat (the mix/scene removing things *is* the score).

**Escalation ladder:**
1. Scattered debris, unremarkable at first — could be anything.
2. A piece of identifiable mech armor plating, confirming what they're tracking.
3. Personal effects — a boot, a comms headset — the first hint this isn't a clean recovery.
4. The wreck itself comes into view around the final bend: the mech, down on its side or slumped against the terrain, unmoving.

**Exit condition:** first visual on the wreck. Rhodes calls a halt and a cautious approach — something (a rigged trip-line, a warning shot, a glimpse of movement) signals the site isn't as abandoned as it looks.

### 4.3 CONTACT (~12 min) — the mission's centerpiece, not a fight

**Vance (LOCKED design intent, same law as Ms. Idris in M1):** in or near the cockpit, the squad finds Vance's body. **No lingering gore, no slow-motion, no score.** Rhodes logs it procedurally over comms exactly like she logged PALAWAN's calcification in M1 — brief, human, moving on. This should land quietly; it is not the mission's biggest emotional beat, because Achterberg's discovery a few minutes later is.

**Achterberg:** alive, has been surviving alone at the site for over a week, and is armed and clearly ready to use it. **Design law: this is not a combat encounter.** The player never fires on her and she never seriously threatens to kill the player — but it needs to read as a real possibility for a few tense seconds, not a foregone conclusion. Mechanically, this plays as a **de-escalation sequence**, not a firefight:
- The player is asked to do something active — lower their weapon on a prompt, hold position, let an NPC (probably Rhodes) talk while the player visibly doesn't escalate — rather than just watching a cutscene resolve itself.
- Achterberg's dialogue reveals *why* she's this wound tight: this isn't paranoia for its own sake, she's already had to warn off at least one group of scavengers/salvagers poking around a downed military asset in the days since the crash (ties naturally into the retired draft's Meridian-salvage-culture material — worth relocating that flavor here even if the fence mission itself doesn't survive; see §8).
- The turn: Rhodes identifies the unit, uses Vance's name, or otherwise proves they're garrison, not scavengers. Achterberg lowers her weapon. No music sting, no triumphant framing — she doesn't relax, she just stops pointing a gun at people who turned out to actually be looking for her.

**Exit condition:** Achterberg, guarded but no longer hostile, agrees to help get the mech moving — not out of relief, but because leaving it (and Vance) in the mountains isn't something she's willing to do either.

### 4.4 FIRST SEAT (~10 min) — the tutorial, played as necessity

**The problem, stated diegetically:** the mech can't be airlifted out intact and can't be left — it has to walk out under its own power to a extraction point the helicopter can actually reach. It's damaged enough that this isn't simple, and Achterberg, alone, can't fly and run diagnostics/recovery systems at the same time. She needs someone in the second seat.

**The seat (LOCKED intent):** the player climbs into the gunnery/systems position — Vance's seat. The game does not comment on this. No dialogue calls it out as symbolic; the player either notices or doesn't, matching M1's established discipline of never explaining its own quiet wrongness.

**The tutorial, scoped [v2, needs reconciling with M6 — see §5]:**
- Achterberg walks the player through only what's needed to get a damaged mech walking: power-up sequence, basic movement (forward/back/turn), camera/look controls, and reading a simple status/diagnostics readout (so "learning to operate it" has a real system behind it, not just movement).
- **No weapons, no targeting, no sync/clasp UI, no combat verbs of any kind.** This mech isn't fighting today — it's limping.
- Achterberg's teaching voice is the tutorial UI, same pattern as Idris being the carry's nav system in M1 — prefer diegetic instruction over menu popups wherever the two can substitute for each other.
- The walk-out itself is the tutorial's practical exam: navigate the mech through the terrain back to the extraction clearing, Achterberg coaching and reacting in real time (a stumble on rough terrain, a diagnostic warning, a moment where she goes quiet thinking about Vance and has to visibly pull herself back to the task).

**Exit condition / mission end:** the mech, walking rough but upright, reaches the extraction clearing as the transport arrives. No triumphant music — this is closer to M1's ending register (quiet, earned, not celebratory). Achterberg is now, functionally and narratively, the player's copilot going forward. Title card or mission-end treatment, format TBD — recommend matching M1's understatement rather than an unlock-fanfare beat, given the tone this mission has been building.

## 5. Scope conflict — flag for resolution before this gets built

Per your answer, mech tutorial controls start in M2, not M6 as the roadmap currently has it (`PHASE1_ROADMAP.md` lists "Two Hearts (frame tutorial)" as the Act 2 opener). **Recommended split, not yet approved:**

- **M2 teaches:** movement, camera, basic systems/diagnostics — a walking mech, no weapons. Framed entirely as recovery/necessity, not training.
- **M6 keeps:** the sync/clasp UI layer, weapons/combat verbs, the artery power meter, resonance triggers — everything from the bible's §III.2 frame-combat description. M6 becomes "your first real fight in it" rather than "your first time in it at all," which if anything makes Two Hearts's existing design *stronger* — the player already knows how to walk before M6 asks them to fight.

This needs Connor/Shane sign-off since it touches M6, which isn't this doc's mission to unilaterally rewrite — flagging it here rather than assuming it.

## 7. Sensitivity rules in force

- Vance's body: no lingering gore, no slow-motion, no dwelling shot — same standard as Ms. Idris in M1.
- Achterberg's standoff: never plays as a horror beat or a "is she a threat" jump-scare structure. She's a grieving, exhausted, scared person, not a twist antagonist. The tension is real but the framing stays sympathetic to her throughout.
- No quips during either the body discovery or the standoff — Static's tone law from M1 applies here too.

## 8. What got displaced from the original M2 draft — OPEN QUESTION

The retired fence/vigil/pylon/SIRENA mission was carrying real franchise weight that this new M2 doesn't touch. Needs a decision, not made here:

- **The Field Recovery Tag system's introduction** (Cpl. Ojo's tag) — this was M2's original vehicle for teaching the collectible-memorial system. Needs a new mission to debut in.
- **Kettler, the hymn-wobble line, Bellringer tinnitus** — seeds Q5 and a T3 ledger entry.
- **Aveiro, the pre-dated Meridian salvage tags** — seeds Q10, pays off M8/M13. This is a significant plant; losing its mission slot without a replacement risks a continuity gap the bible's ledger depends on.
- **The Hermit Fleet zero-Emergence codex bulletin** — pays T4.
- **First SIRENA sighting** — pays Q4, M10's mirror, Y1 S4. Arguably the highest-value single beat in the retired draft.
- **The shoal-stray probe encounter** — was meant to amortize into M3's shoal fight (build the creature once, multiply it later). If this doesn't happen in some mission before M3, M3 loses its own setup beat.

**Recommendation, not a decision:** the cleanest fix is probably folding some or all of this into M3 ("Shoal" per the roadmap) rather than inventing a whole new mission slot, since M3 already needs the shoal-stray content and several of these beats (SIRENA, the tags) don't depend on the specific fence-line setting — they could relocate to wherever M3 ends up being set. But that's a real structural call for the campaign, not something to decide inside M2's own doc. Flagging it and moving on.
