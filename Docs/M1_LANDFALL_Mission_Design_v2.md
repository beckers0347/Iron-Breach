## 0. What changed from v1 (read this first)

The original four-beat draft (QUIET → DREAD → BURST → AFTERMATH) treated PALAWAN as a Class C kaiju the player's squad never fights and never could fight — it surfaces, does its thing, and calcifies un-engaged. That is no longer the plan.

**[v2] The core change:** M1 now escalates through two distinct kaiju contacts, not one:

1. A handful of **Class D** kaiju hit the district first. The garrison — infantry plus its one on-base mech — struggles but holds its own. This is a *winnable* fight, and it's meant to feel like one: the player's first taste of "we've got this."
2. Just as that fight looks won, **PALAWAN surfaces — reclassified from Class C to Class B.** This is a real step up: Class B doctrine says infantry cannot break its armor and it takes multiple mechs to put down. The garrison has exactly one. It goes anyway.

This changes the five-act structure (from the build-status doc) from a generic "Class C win / Class A loss" template into a specific, named story: the same mech and the same two pilots carry the audience from the Class D fight straight into losing to PALAWAN. Acts I and II are untouched — they're already built. Acts III, IV, and V are rewritten below.

## 2. What M1 must accomplish

**Narrative (LOCKED, updated **[v2]** where noted):**
1. Establish the war at human height — kaiju as weather with intent, not boss content (Act 1 design intent).
2. Deliver hammer 1: Ms. Idris — "small, unscored, definitional" (§XIV.1). Loss before lore (L3).
3. Earn the callsign FERRYMAN over her stretcher — "the one who carries people across."
4. **[v2]** Stage the campaign's first quiet wrongness: PALAWAN, having thrown the garrison's mech into the mountains and taken everything the humans had, stops on its own two blocks short of the hospital and calcifies (seed for Q3 / Raid 1). The wrongness is no longer "it was never fought" — it's "it won, and stopped anyway."
5. **[v2]** Establish the human cost of the mech program early: Vance and Achterberg are the first named pilots the player meets, and the mission ends with one of them presumed dead and the mech itself gone, missing in the mountains — a second, unresolved loss ledger entry alongside PALAWAN's calcification.
6. Lore payload: Emergence 101; tide-mark culture (the glimpsed kneeling frame).

**Mechanical:**
1. Teach movement, interaction, gunplay/ADS, sprint-mantle traversal, and the carry — with the carry as the player's defining verb (L1: the world introduced through what the player *does*).
2. **[v2]** Teach the escalation ladder in play, not in text: infantry-solvable (Class D) → mech-solvable in theory, not in practice (Class B) → nothing solves it (the mech loses too). Three data points, no dialogue needed to explain any of them.
3. Establish objective grammar for the whole game: **extraction counts on-screen, kills don't** (LOCKED).
4. Teach the conventional-arms ceiling honestly: the garrison's weapons buy minutes, never kills. **[v2]** Now the mech teaches the same lesson one tier up: it buys the retreat time, not the win.

**Franchise:**
1. The Nine enters the game in minute one via Static's podcast (LOCKED — L27 fingerprint).
2. Seed glow-veins/arteries (pays T4) and calcification-as-function (pays Raid 1/Q3) without comment.
3. Establish tone law in play: no quips during casualties; the camera returns to human height.
4. **[v2]** Plant a mid/late-campaign hook: the mech and (per current assumption, see §3a) Vance's body are lost in the mountains outside Carrowgate, unrecovered at mission end. This is a deliberate loose thread for a later mission — do not resolve it here.

### 3a. New characters — **[v2]** the mech crew

The garrison's single on-base mech deploys against PALAWAN with a two-person crew. Both are new to the cast; both need Connor/Shane sign-off before they're written into the Director scripts.

**Cpl. Desmond "Junction" Vance — gunnery/systems seat.**
Former dockworker, enlisted late — older than most Wet Boots, treats the mech like heavy equipment rather than a weapon ("she's a crane that hits back"). Talks in checklists under pressure. He's the one who named the mech something unglamorous and workmanlike, which nobody's bothered to change. Current assumption (flag, not locked — see §13 open question 7): Vance is the crew member lost when PALAWAN throws the mech into the mountains at the end of Act V — his death should read as sudden and procedural, cut off mid-checklist, contrasting deliberately with Idris's quiet, intimate death earlier in the same act.

**Sgt. Priya Achterberg — "Ledger" — pilot/movement seat.**
Precise, controlled, actually flying the frame while Vance runs its weapons — she's the reason the mech (and anyone in it) survives being thrown at all, an instinctive save rather than a heroic one. Quiet rather than talkative; her callsign is because she keeps mental score of everything that's gone wrong on a mission and never says it out loud until debrief. Survives the mountain crash, becomes the player's copilot for the remainder of the campaign starting the mission this thread is picked back up. Losing Vance mid-fight and still having to fly the mech out is her defining beat — she doesn't get to grieve in the moment, which is what makes her worth spending a campaign with afterward.

## 4. Structure — five acts

**[v2]** This revision retires the original four-beat naming (QUIET/DREAD/BURST/AFTERMATH) in favor of the five-act structure already used by the build-status doc and already reflected in the codebase (`Act1BarracksDirector`, `Act2EscalationDirector`, and the planned `Act3`/`Act4`/`Act5` directors). The original beat content is preserved inside the acts below — nothing narrative from v1 is lost, it's re-hung on the new frame plus the Class D/Class B rewrite.

### Act I — "Barracks Intro" ✅ built, unchanged

*(= original QUIET beat, "Stand the Watch")*

04:12, Carrowgate watch room. Night-shift garrison routine as diegetic tutorial: movement, interact, dry-fire ADS drill on the wall. Static theorizes the Green Tomb hum matches the anomaly's frequency signature — first time he's been right about the Nine, and nobody's listening. Rhodes enters; Comms reports a climbing micro-seismic cluster; Rhodes calls it a birthquake precursor and gives the stand-to order. Closes on a narration beat foreshadowing how wrong "supporting" the evac is about to feel.

No combat. Already placed and wired in `CarrowGateGarrison` (`AAct1BarracksDirector`).

### Act II — "An Eerie Escalation" ✅ built, unchanged

*(= original DREAD beat, "The Birds Are Gone")*

Auto-chains off Act I. The fireteam moves into the district to marshal a pre-dawn evacuation (buses loading, wardens with paddles, civilians in coats over pajamas) — door-knocking and escort, no combat, onboarding the extraction verb before it has stakes. Escalation ladder, in order: birdless sky, a K9 refusing to work, glow-veins surfacing through the asphalt (a child evacuee reaches for one, pulled back — "the flowers," planted a decade early). No music — the mix removing things *is* the score. Ends when the road swells like an inhale, sirens change tone, and Rhodes calls contact directly beneath them.

No combat. Already placed and wired in `CarrowGateGarrison` (`AAct2EscalationDirector`), chained off Act I.

### Act III — "Contact" **[v2] rewritten** — Class D, winnable

*(replaces the old "Class C, winnable" placeholder from the build-status doc; folds in the original BURST beat's route-clearing content)*

3–4 Class D kaiju (stock SKITTERLING/CARAPACE, no new species asset needed per the codex update) surface near the garrison perimeter as the district collapse begins. This is the mission's earned win: infantry holds the line, Rhodes calls targets, the garrison's one mech — **Vance and Achterberg's first appearance, introduced here as capable and a little cocky** — mops up what the squad can't. Route-clearing gunplay is framed as logistics, not combat: shooting to open paths (a snagged cable-stay, a demo charge on a fallen facade, called shots on a leaking gas valve), same grammar as v1's original burst content.

**Design intent:** the player should feel like the fight is basically won. That feeling is the setup for Act IV, not padding before it.

Dialogue beats (contact klaxon, mid-fight callouts, "clear," Vance/Achterberg banter establishing them before the mission needs the player to care) bracket the fight via `ScriptedEventTag`s the same way Acts I/II do. Spawn/kill logic lives on an `AIBKaijuSpawner` instance (`MinClass = MaxClass = ClassD`, 3–4 concurrent), not in the Director.

**Exit condition:** the last Class D kaiju goes down or breaks off. Radio chatter starts sounding like a win. Then the ground swells again — bigger this time.

### Act IV — "Deep Water" **[v2] rewritten** — PALAWAN's Class B reveal

*(replaces the old generic "unnamed Class A surfaces" beat; this is where the original BURST beat's eruption content lands, recontextualized)*

**The eruption (carried over from v1, LOCKED in spirit):** PALAWAN surfaces mid-district over roughly ninety seconds of scripted geology — buildings shrug apart, the glow-veins go arterial-bright, and an 82-meter animal stands up out of a residential block, dwarfing everything the Act III fight just proved the garrison could handle. District collapse continues/escalates.

**Objective grammar (LOCKED, unchanged):** the HUD still shows only **CIVILIANS EXTRACTED**, counting up. No kill counter, ever, for either kaiju class.

**Infantry side (unchanged from v1's gun-line beat):** the fireteam reaches the sea-wall deterrent battery. Bellringer acoustic pieces plus the garrison's siege gun — the player crews the battery for one sequence, "ringing" PALAWAN's lead limb to make it flinch, buying the convoy seconds at a chokepoint. The siege gun fires beside them and accomplishes nothing but noise and spall. Rule unchanged: **the flinch is a redirect, never damage. No health bar exists for infantry's part of this fight.**

**Mech side [v2, new]:** Rhodes commits the garrison's one mech — Vance and Achterberg — against PALAWAN, because Class B doctrine says it takes multiple mechs and they have exactly one. This is not treated as a plan that might work; it's treated as buying time, same grammar as the deterrent battery, one tier up. The mech gets real hits in — it's not a token gesture — but PALAWAN outclasses it visibly: armor doesn't break, and after a sustained exchange the mech is clearly losing. This is the fight's centerpiece and should get real screen time; it's the first time the player has seen the frame program's toys and the first time they've watched them fail.

**Exit condition:** Rhodes calls the fall-back — not a squad retreat this time, a full garrison withdrawal. The mech covers it, still fighting, as the act ends.

### Act V — "Full Retreat" **[v2] rewritten** — carries over the original AFTERMATH beat plus the mech loss

*(the build-status doc had already moved the carry sequence here from the original "Act IV" slot; this revision keeps that and adds the mech-toss beat ahead of it)*

**The toss [v2, new, LOCKED intent]:** covering the withdrawal, the mech takes one hit too many — PALAWAN backhands or grabs-and-throws it clear off the battlefield, into the high ground/mountains outside Carrowgate. This is the mission's second-biggest spectacle beat after the eruption and should be shot as a single unbroken, horrifying arc — not a cut-away. Per current assumption (§3a, flagged for confirmation), this is where Vance is lost; Achterberg gets the mech (and herself) down, hurt but alive. **Neither pilot's fate needs to be confirmed on-screen in M1** — comms lose the mech in the mountains and it's not recovered by mission end. This is a deliberate plant for a later mission, not a loose end to tidy up here.

**The carry (carried over from v1, LOCKED, non-negotiable):** the player carries Ms. Idris 400 meters through the dust toward the hospital muster.
- **No stamina bar. No timer. No fail state. No score.** The carry is a rite, not a challenge. Walking pace is forced. The HUD clears entirely.
- **Her voice is the waypoint system** — she navigates her own rescue ("Left at the bakery, love..."). No marker, no compass.
- She talks to keep them both calm — small, specific, human. Then, mid-street, she stops. No sting, no slow-motion. The player walks the last hundred meters with no guide, in silence; the level design must make that stretch legible without her.
- Per §XIX.3: her death is aftermath and memory, never gore.

**The naming (LOCKED, unchanged):** at the hospital muster, the squad takes her weight onto a stretcher. Rhodes asks, procedurally, who brought her in. Bricks coins it gentle on purpose: *Ferryman.* Static repeats it once, no joke in him. The callsign appears on the player's HUD tag as the line lands.

**The first quiet wrongness [v2, reframed]:** as the squad works, PALAWAN — having just taken the garrison's mech and everything else thrown at it — slows two blocks short of the hospital, folds down onto itself, and begins to calcify. It just stops, after winning. Nobody on comms can explain it and nobody tries; Rhodes logs it in procedural language and moves on. **No character comments on the strangeness. Ever.** The wrongness now reads as "it won and quit anyway" rather than v1's "it was never even challenged" — arguably a stronger hook for the same seed (function, not rampage → Raid 1/Q3).

**The glimpse (LOCKED, unchanged):** on the walk back along the sea-wall at first light, one wide shot: a Caryatid kneeling in the shallows, crew hand-painting a fresh ring onto its greave. Bricks delivers tide-mark culture in one line — **[PROPOSAL]** "Rings are people carried out. Nobody paints kills." **[v2 note]** this beat now lands even harder with a lost mech crew fresh in the player's mind — consider letting the player's eyeline linger on the ring itself, since the game hasn't yet told them whether one gets painted for Vance.

**The evac [v2.1, new]:** the squad boards a garrison transport helicopter off the hospital's landing apron for evacuation to the fallback position — the mission's final beat before the title card, and the connective tissue into M2. As the helicopter lifts out over the district and banks toward the mountains inland, the player has a window seat view of the terrain the mech was thrown into. **Design intent:** brief, wordless if possible — a distant plume of smoke or a scar in the treeline, gone in a few seconds, no dialogue calling attention to it (matches the mission's established rule of not narrating its own wrongness — see the calcification beat above). One optional line only, and only if silence reads as *missed* rather than *restrained* in playtest: Achterberg, still on comms, quiet: "...that's the ridge. That's where we came down." This is the seed M2 pays off — the squad (and the player) knows roughly where to look before M2 begins.

**Mission end [PROPOSAL, updated **[v2.1]**]:** title card — IRON BREACH — over the kneeling silhouette (unchanged), now immediately preceded by the helicopter's climb-out shot above rather than a hard cut from the sea-wall. Cut to black on the first bell-toll of a distant frame footfall, unchanged.

## 5. Encounter design — Class D contact and PALAWAN

**Class D contact (Act III) [v2, new content, not detailed in v1]:** stock species, no new build required — see codex §5a. Treat as a genuine, if minor, combat encounter: real threat, real feedback, a fight the player and the garrison mech visibly win together before the tone turns. This is the only combat in the mission infantry can actually claim credit for.

**PALAWAN, updated:** still not a boss in the traditional sense — no player-facing health bar, no targeting of the player, no AI aggro system for infantry's benefit. What changes in v2 is that it now has an opponent that *can* meaningfully hit it (the mech), which the Director layer needs to track independently of anything infantry does. Build terms, updated from v1:

- **What it is in build terms:** a scripted level actor for everything infantry-facing (spline-driven locomotion, sequencer-owned animation, collision that world-destruction reacts to, deterrent-flinch montage) — this part is unchanged from v1's design and should still cost the same to build.
- **[v2, new]** The mech engagement is a separate, bounded combat sequence layered on top of the same actor — likely its own short state machine or sequencer beat (mech gets hits in → PALAWAN registers no meaningful damage → escalates → grab/throw). This does **not** need a general-purpose kaiju combat AI; it's still scripted, just scripted for two participants instead of one. Flag for Connor: confirm whether this warrants a light `IDamageableInterface` implementation purely for VFX/feedback on the mech's hits (no real health loss) or stays fully non-interactive on PALAWAN's side, matching the "it wins regardless of input" intent.

**Why this is still right, not just cheap:** the fiction now requires that infantry cannot fight a Class B at all, that even the garrison's mech can't beat it alone, and that its stopping afterward is still unexplained. The first kaiju that can be *touched* still can't be *beaten* — Act 2's real frame content (multiple mechs, Tandem warfare) then lands as "we brought enough, this time" rather than just "a bigger gun."

## 7. Objective & UI grammar (game-wide precedents set here)

- Extractions count up on the HUD; the counter never decrements; kills are never numbered anywhere in the game (LOCKED).
- The carry clears the HUD entirely. Precedent: the game removes UI at its most important moments (recurs at M9 Ward 9, M14, the M15 aftermath).
- Objective text is radio-procedural, in Rhodes' register — orders, not quest-speak.
- Deaths: player down in Act III/IV → squad pickup or checkpoint restart. Checkpoints at beat boundaries plus one mid-Act-IV. Scripted losses (Idris, Vance) are narrative, never fail states.
- The carry cannot fail and cannot be skipped; abandoning it soft-resets to its start.
- **[v2]** The mech-toss beat is likewise non-interactive/non-failable — it's a cutscene-weight sequencer beat the player watches or, at most, reacts to cosmetically (camera, maybe a "brace" prompt), never something they can prevent or lose. Losing it *is* the point.

## 9. Audio / visual direction

- **Palette:** pre-dawn blue-black → sodium streetlight amber → the veins' gold-green → grey dust dawn. The mission ends in the first true daylight of the game.
- **Score discipline [PROPOSAL]:** no music until the tide-mark glimpse. **[v2]** consider whether the mech-toss beat earns a rare, brief exception (a single sting on impact) — flag for the audio pass rather than deciding here.
- **The Nine in the mix:** diegetic in the podcast; thereafter buried sub-audibly in the ambient bed.
- **Scale language:** PALAWAN is shot from below or in partial frame for the whole mission — never a clean full-body hero shot, **[v2]** including during the mech fight; the player should feel its scale relative to the mech (which they *can* read clearly) rather than get a clean silhouette of PALAWAN itself.
- **Sound signature:** birthquake as sub-bass pressure felt before heard; calcification as a slow mineral *ticking*.

## 10. Sensitivity rules in force (§XIX.3, contractual)

- Ms. Idris: aftermath and memory, no lingering gore, no death rattle, no slow-mo. Children are never on-screen casualties.
- **[v2]** Vance's death (if confirmed per §13 open question 7) follows the same law: no lingering gore, no slow-mo. It should read as sudden and procedural (cut off mid-checklist) rather than graphic — the contrast with Idris's quiet death is tonal, not a difference in how explicit either is allowed to be.
- No quips during casualties. Static's humor exists so the sirens can end it.
- Real nations stay offscreen; Carrowgate is a fictional primary.
